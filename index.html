1. The Post-Coda Hypothesis

  • OC *-s > MC qùshēng 去聲 ‘departing tone’ (Haudricourt [1954] 2017)
  • OC *-ʔ > MC shǎngshēng 上聲 ‘rising tone’ (Pulleyblank 1962b; Mei 1970)
  • Supported by Chinese loans into Vietic languages, transcription evidence, and residual glottalization of shǎngshēng in modern dialects.
  • Today’s questions:
    1. How well are OC complex obstruent codas, *-Ps, supported by the evidence?
    2. Can we refine the chronology of coda simplification?

1.1. New Distinctions Implied by the Post-Coda Hypothesis

  • The Post-Coda Hypothesis implies new distinctions between rhymes that end in vowels or glides (G) + *-s and stops (P) + *-s
    • Older reconstructions, here Li Fang-Kuei (1980) and Wang Li ([1960] 1989), had a three-way distinction;post-codas introduce a fourth (row 2 vs. 3).
    • Post-codas after consonantal sonorants (nasal codas and *-r) do not introduce new distinctions.
    • Can divide into three classes (see below)
    • Class III is remarkable because the jì 祭 group, Vts, corresponds entirely to qùshēng words and contrasts sharply with gē 歌, Vj(s). No parallel among classes I & II in traditional reconstructions.

1.2. Hypotheses of Simplification

  • Generally agreed that *-ps and *-ts merge before the Shījīng 詩經 was compiled.
    • Usually treated as assimilation to *-ts
    • One possible vestige of separate *-ps rhyme in Ode 255 蕩 3A (Baxter 1992, 310)
  • *-(w)ks & *-(w)s also rhyme in Shījīng, implying simplification of class I was complete.
    • Merger acknowledged in the first formulation of the post-coda hypothesis (Haudricourt [1954] 2017, 30).
  • *-ts and *-js merge after OC *-aj (gē 歌 group) monophthongization (Baxter 1992, 312).
    • Explains why distinctions among class III codas are easier to discern than class II—MC reflexes different.
    • *-aj monophthongization happened in the 1 century AD (Coblin 1987, 100–101).
    • Starostin suggests merger also occurred earlier after *i and *ə
  • *-s debuccalizes to *-h in post coda position following obstruent coda simplification.
    • Baxter & Sagart (2014) place this change after *-aj monophthongization.
    • Starostin ([1989] 2010, 295) places it much earlier since it does not affect *-ps and *-ts, which he believes merged as *-ć [tɕ].

2. Proposed Modifications

  • Most of the above were not sound changes, but rather synchronic strategies to avoid complex codas in word-level phonology.
  • After sonorant codas, the qùshēng post-coda was *-h mirroring *-ʔ.
  • Elsewhere, we have *-h (OCBS *-V(k)s), *-s (OCBS *-V[t/p]s), *-wh (OCBS *-Vw(k)s)
    • Following Starostin, *-s and *-jh do not contrast after *i, *ə. After back vowel breaking (3), the distinction is lost in class II rimes 脂微, i.e. *-us > *-wəjh [-wɨɕ].
  • In post-coda position, *-h and *-ʔ could also be interpreted as phonation features of sonorant codas.

2.1. Implications

  • Most uncertainty involving complex obstruent codas in the OCBS system— e.g. *-(k)-s, *-[t/p]-s—can be eliminated.
  • Qùshēng derivation does not always surface as a discrete segmental suffix.
    • Cf. coda simplification involving 兒 diminutives in Mandarin (-r) and Wú (-n), Yuè bin³jam¹ 變音 ‘changed tones’, Kuki-Chin coda alternations in verb stem allomorphy (I ⁓ II).
    • Pre-OC form/source(s) left indeterminate (but see Button 2011; Jacques 2016 for suggestions).
  • Like Schuessler (2007), not all cases of my *-s & *-h necessarily go back to qùshēng derivation.
  • Debuccalization (6), a synchronic constraint here, did not occur after *-aj monophthongization (4).
    • Confirmed by loans into Vietic and Korean, as we shall see.
  • Coronal coda simplification (5) was not a matter of *-ts weakening to *-js, but rather a change of *-s > *-jh [ɕ].
    • Fell into complementary distribution after *-aj monophthongization, but still phonetically distinct centuries later.
    • Reanalyzed as [-s] in pharyngealized (“type A”) syllables and [-ɕ] in plain (“type B”)

3. Reconciling *-Ps with Traditional Sources of OC

3.1. Shījīng Rhymes

For class II (脂微), only two sequences of unmixed *-jh (OCBS *-js) in SJ.

  1. Ode 212.3 小雅・大田
    彼有不穫穉，此有不斂穧。
    彼有遺秉，此有滯​穗，伊寡婦之​利。

    Baxter (1992) 微 -ɨjs, 脂/質 -ij/ts

  1. Ode 254.7 大雅・板
    無俾城壞，無獨斯畏。

    Baxter (1992) 微 -ujs

  • Hard to establish contrast with *-s (OCBS -[t]s) because of uncertainty.
  • *-jh vs. *-s are established by morphological pairs, then extrapolated used xiéshēng connections.
    • 穉 MC drijH ‘late-ripening grain’ ⁓ 遲 MC drij ‘late, slow’
    • 畏 MC ’jw+jH ‘hold in awe’ ⁓ 威 ’jw+j ‘overawe’
    • Relative age xiéshēng complicate these extrapolations.

See Wang Li ([1963] 1989) for a comprehensive study of class II *-jh (his V(ː)i 平/上 > 去) vs. *-s (his Vːt 長入 > 去) using Shījīng rhymes and conventional xiéshēng evidence.

3.2. Xiéshēng — 貴

  • OCBS takes GSR 540 臾 (top part of 䝿 = 貴) to write *-uj words
  • Prob. because
    • 遺 MC ywij < *juj (OCBS *[ɢ](r)uj) ‘leave behind’
    • 遺 MC ywijH < *jujh (OCBS *[ɢ](r)uj-s) ‘turn over to’
  • But cf.
    • 匱 MC gwijH < *gus ‘to run out, come to end’ (Ode 247 孝子不～)
      technically would expect MC gjwɨjH
    • 屈 MC gjut < *gut ‘to exhaust’ (Sūnzǐ 2 攻城則力～)
  • 匱 gwijH ‘to run out’ with GSR 540, as ⟨蕢⟩, attested in the Yínquèshān mss. c. 2nd century BC.
    • 守令十三篇・4 王兵: 有積委，久而不～
    • Predates *-aj monophthongization

3.3. Xiéshēng — 次

  • GSR 555 次 writes MC píngshēng words, suggesting it wrote *-ij(s) in OCBS
    • 咨 tsij ‘consult’, 資 tsij ‘resources’, 餈 dzij ‘rice cake’
  • 次 tshijH itself is qùshēng
  • In Chǔ mss., 次 is written with GSR 554 𠂔 (cf. 姊 tsijX) and GSR 399 即 (cf. 節 tset), but they write different meanings (Xuān 2013, 71–72).
    • ⟨𠂔⟩ ‘position (in sequence)’ 日月星辰，用交亂進，而莫得其～ (清華三《芮良夫𪫠》23)
    • ⟨⿱宀𠂔⟩ ‘camp (of armies)’ 九三：旅焚其～ (上博三《周易》53)
    • ⟨即⟩ ‘second, next’ 太上下知有之，其～親譽之 (郭店《老子》丙1)
  • Distinct words with phonological difference? Regional/temporal difference? “Equivalent phonophorics” (Park 2016)?
  • Xuān Jiàncōng observes sense 1 written as 次 is an innovation, attested in Shuìhǔdì c. 217 BC.
    • Predates *-aj monophthongization regardless of analysis.

4. Post-Coda Typology in OC’s Neighbors

  • Languages with sonorants + glottal pre/post-codas typically do not also permit other complex clusters that involve segments specified for place like -s.
    • Gyalrong languages are complicated. Tone realization linked with syllable final glottals, but some inflectional suffixes permitted after: Zbu /-ŋ/ 1sg; Situ /-tɕ ⁓ <j>/ du (Zhang 2016, 68–69).
  • Conversely, languages that permit complex obstruent codas do not have glottal post-codas.
  • Typology of conventional OC post-codas unusual. *-ʔ only occurs after vowels and sonorant codas. *-s can end any syllable.
  • Pre-OC **-MC > *-Mʔ, *-Mh probably driven by the process.

5. The Vietic Evidence

5.1. Proto-Vietic *-s & *-h

  • Maspero (1912) organized the tones of modern Vietnamese (Vn) into three categories:
    • A ngang(1)-huyền(2); B hỏi(1)-ngã(2); C sắc(1)-nặng(2)
  • B comes from final fricatives *-h and *-ɕ in Proto-Austroasiatic, pAA (Sidwell 2024).
    • Haudricourt ([1954] 2018) believed these fricative codas originated from *-s. This inspired *-s in Old Chinese.
  • C comes from *-ʔ, other stop codas, and glottalized sonorant codas.
  • Conservative Vietic languages (Chứt-Arem, Tavung-Malieng, etc.) retain final fricative and glottal codas, giving a comparative basis for tonogenesis.

  • In ESV-1, OC *-Vh, *-Vs, *-Vjh correspond to B; but *-Mh corresponds to A
    • This is because pV did not have *-Mh codas.
    • Alves (2018) argues all cases of qùshēng corresponding to B are from ESV-1. This is an overgeneralization.
  • In ESV-2 OC *-Vh and a few cases of *-Vs, *-Vjh correspond to ESV tone A.
    • e.g. 鋸 kjoH < OC *kah, Vn cưa (A1) ‘saw (tool)’; 帶 tajH < OC *ʼtas, Vn dải (B1)⁓​đai (A1) ‘belt’
    • Reflects loss of fricative codas in Chinese in a middle stage before Vietic tonogenesis.
  • Only four cases of OC *-Mh correspond to ESV tone B in Alves’ data.
    • e.g. 共 gjowngH < OC *ɡoŋh ‘together’, Vn cũng (B2) ‘also’

5.3. Loan Examples & Chronology

  • OC *-Mh (excluding *-jh [ɕ]) / pV -M
    • 劍 kjomH < OC *(s)kamh; VN gươm (A1), Sách [təkɨəm¹] < pV *t-kɨəm ‘sword’
  • OC *-(M)ʔ / pV *-(M)ʔ
    • 染 nyemX < OC *n[o]mʔ; Vn nhuộm (C2), Arem [ɲùːmʔ] < pV *ɲɔːmʔ ‘dye’
  • OC *-h / pV *-h
    • 豆 duwH < OC *ʼdoh; VN đỗ (B2), Thavung [tuh²] < pV *duh ‘beans’
  • OC *-s, *-jh / pV *-s
    • 芥 kaejH < OC *ʼkras; VN cải (B1), Thavung [kajh¹] < pV *kaːs ‘mustard’


  • Two loans involving *-h and *-jh predate OC *-aj monophthongization:
  • 墓 muh < OC *ʼʔmah; Vn mả (B1), Sách [mah¹] < pV *-mah ‘tomb’
    • OC *-a rose to *-o in open syllables before *-aj > *-a / pharyngeal (A)
  • 寄 kjeH < OC *kajh; VN gửi/gởi (B1), Arem [pakəːjʔ] < pV *p-kəːs ‘send, entrust’
    • Would expect *kajh > *keh and *-h in pV if *-aj monophthongization had occurred

6.3. Korean

  • No segmental reflection of OC *-(M)h, but some *-k words were loaned with -h:
    • 褥 nyowk < *nok, MK zywoh ᅀᅭᇂ > yo 요 ‘bed-roll’
    • 尺 tshyek < *[tʰ]ak, MK cah 잫 > ca 자 ‘ruler’
  • *-s, *Vjh (II)
    • 芥 keajH < *’kras, MK kas 갓 ‘mustard’
    • 味 mj+jH < *məs, MK mas 맛 ‘taste’
    • 篦/枇 bijH < *bih [-ɕ], MK pis 빗 ‘comb’
  • *Vjh (III) 磨 maH < *’majh, MK máy 매〮 ‘millstone’ from 訓蒙字会 Hwunmong cahwoy (1527)
    • Predates spread of *-aj monophthongization in eastern China
    • Original *-h prob. already developed into tone, cf. MK may 매 ‘grind’.
    • -s in K. maystol 맷돌 ‘millstone’ was a genitive marker (pace Zhèngzhāng 2003, 212).
      • Special thanks to Han Gyeol for his insights into MK morphology!
  • Correspondence is difficult to establish because reconstruction of Old Korean is still uncertain.

7.1. OC *-h in transcriptions

  • OC *-h does not usually correspond to coda segments in early transcriptions
    • T 98.992.2 舍利弗 BTD *śɑH.liH.put, Skt. śariput(ra)
    • T 224.426.1 阿惟越致 BTD *ɑ.wii.wat.tiH, Gd. *​avivat(in⁓ika), Skt. avivart(in⁓ika)
    • Hànshū 高附 pre-BTD *kɑu.buɔH = Kabul (Pulleyblank 1962b, 223)
    • but 謝 (c. 90 ad) pre-BTD *ziɑ = šāhi (Pulleyblank 1962b, 221)
    • T 13.236.1 梵 BTD *bamH, Gd. braṃm(a), Skt. brahm(ā)
  • OC *-i[h/s] does not correspond to sibilant codas for 利 lijH (cf. pBG *jiH) and 致 trijH
    • see above, cf. 貳 in T 224.435.1 阿迦貳吒 BTD *ɑ.ka.ńiC.tɹɑH, Skt. akaniṣṭha
    • Retention of *-ih vs. *-is or conditioned split after retroflex initials?

8. Conclusion

  1. Revised Old Chinese Coda Inventory

    */ m n ŋ   r j w ∅  
      mʔ nʔ ŋʔ   rʔ jʔ wʔ ʔ  
      mh nh ŋh s rh jh [ɕ] wh h  
      p t k       wk   /
  • Rhyming evidence and the phonotactics of neighboring languages with glottal post-codas militate against the reconstruction of complex obstruent codas, *-Ps, in OC.
    • Apart from changes related to *-aj monophthongization, the pre-tonogenesis Hàn situation can be projected back without modification.
  • Reordering of the following changes
    • Debuccalization of **-s and obstruent coda simplification complete before OC
    • Coronal coda simplification (merger of *-s and *-jh) in progress before *-aj monophthongization.
  • Difference in opinion of how to reconstruct OC
    • Distinctions reconstructed to account for archaic morphological and xiéshēng alternations should be confirmed by patterns observed in rhymed texts or later phonological systems.
